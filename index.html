About

We wanted a realistic VR badminton simulator that could be played among friends without the need for real equipment or a court. We wanted to make it as realistic as possible using accurate rules, physics, and playing conditions.

Background
  • A friend came to UMD to play badminton but was denied entry due to the service desk being closed.
  • Difficulty accessing facilities to play sports that require large spaces and extra equipment.
  • Due to Covid, at-home exercise has greatly increased over the past few years, creating a large market demand.
  • A new generation of adults growing up with a nostalgia for movement-tracking games such as Wii Sports and Wii Sports Resort.
Techniques & Approaches
  • Used scientific papers — including The Physics of Badminton by C. Cohen et al. — to obtain accurate measurements of how badminton birdies interact.
  • Employed the Unity particle system to replicate weather and impact-based birdie movement.
  • Used the Meta XR Building Blocks to track hands and hand-grabbing, tying different grabbing actions to distinct purposes.

Court Logic

The court accurately counts scores, manages game states, automatically switches serving sides, keeps score across multi-match games, and enforces serving faults — with and without a redo option.

Zone Detection

BirdieZoneTrigger uses colliders placed across the court with a serialized enum variable unique to each zone, giving the GameManager the exact zone the birdie landed in for serve validation. A special NetTrigger uses two colliders to detect both net contact and net clearance, ensuring all scored hits legitimately crossed the net.

Player Tracking

BirdieZoneTrigger components double as player position tracking, working alongside a PlayerIdentity script that identifies which side the human player is on. Only objects tagged Shuttlecock and Player can trigger these zones.

Game Manager

Enforces game states and serving sides consistent with real badminton rules, tracks score per game and across matches, and allows the player to configure match count and fault rules. Physical invisible walls surround the court, containing birdies inside and players to their respective sides.

Results
CMSC498F Final Project Proposal

The project produced a working framework encompassing court logic, physics simulation, weather effects, and hand-tracking integration — establishing a solid foundation for a fully playable VR badminton experience.

Shortcomings
  • Missing core interaction: Birdie and racket interactions remained unreliable. After several attempts using various methods, the racket could never consistently hit the birdie, which also limited racket variety.
  • Feature integration: The team never combined all individual components into a single unified scene.
  • Lack of gameplay: No game modes or functional AI agents were completed in time.
Future Work

Both team members worked on individual parts of the project but were unfortunately unable to combine them in time. Future goals include completing the remaining base mechanics, adding more game modes, expanding equipment and environmental variety, and improving overall user immersion.

Finishing the AI opponent is also a priority, enabling players to have actual competitive games within the simulation.
